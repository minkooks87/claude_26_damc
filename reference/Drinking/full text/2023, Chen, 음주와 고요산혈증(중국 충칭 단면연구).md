## Abstract


### Objective

To investigate the relationship between alcohol consumption and hyperuricaemia (HUA), we conducted a study based on a large population.


### Setting

Cross-sectional study.


### Participants

A total of 20 833 participants aged 30–79 years were enrolled in the China Multi-Ethnic Cohort, Chongqing region.


### Outcomes

The serum level of uric acid, fasting blood glucose and blood lipids were tested. Basic demographic statistics such as age, gender, marital status, education level, family annual income and the detail information of alcohol consumption were collected using a standardised questionnaire.


### Results

After controlling for potential confounders, compared with participants who never consumed alcohol, participants who drank 3–5 days per week had the highest risk of HUA (OR: 1.51, 95% CI: 1.25 to 1.82) and those who drank alcohol harmfully had the highest risk of HUA (OR: 1.81, 95% CI: 1.41 to 2.32). In addition, we found that those who drank moderately had no significant association with risk of HUA. However, among men, compared with participants who never consumed alcohol, those who drank moderately was also a risk factor of HUA (OR: 1.23, 95% CI: 1.03 to 1.46) and those who drank alcohol harmfully had the highest risk of HUA (OR: 2.13, 95% CI: 1.64 to 2.78). Compared with participants who drank alcohol moderately, the OR (95% CI) for those who drank alcohol harmfully had the highest risk of HUA was 1.88 (1.42 to 2.48), and the corresponding OR (95% CI) for each level increment in the degree of alcohol consumption was 1.22 (1.12 to 1.33). Among men, compared with participants who drank alcohol moderately, those who drank alcohol harmfully had the highest risk of HUA (OR: 1.93, 95% CI: 1.45 to 2.57), as well as the corresponding OR (95% CI) for each level increment in the degree of alcohol consumption was 1.24 (1.13 to 1.35).


### Conclusion

This study suggested that the frequency and degree of alcohol consumption may be the risk factors for HUA, especially in males.


## STRENGTHS AND LIMITATIONS OF THIS STUDY

- The current study included large sample size and adjusted for a broad range of confounding factors.
- This epidemiological study provided some insights into the association between alcohol consumption and hyperuricaemia.
- As a cross-sectional survey, it cannot prove causality, but it preliminarily explored the influence of the frequency and degree of alcohol consumption on hyperuricaemia.
- Standardised Food Frequency Questionnaire was used to obtain participants' drinking information and provide evidence for studying the relationship between alcohol and hyperuricaemia.
- This study focused on Chongqing in the southwest of China, which can represent the demographic characteristics of southwest China.

## Introduction

Hyperuricaemia (HUA) is becoming a common chronic disease with worldwide health and economic burden. According to the white Paper on Trends of HUA and Gout in China 2021, the prevalence rate of HUA in China was 13.3%, and the number of patients was about 177 million.1 A meta-analysis study shows that the prevalence of HUA was 16.4% in Mainland China.2 The prevalence of HUA in the USA was 14.6% and the South Australia was a little higher, reaching 16.6%.3 4 Long-term HUA can cause gout, and if not treated actively, it will cause joint movement disorder. The severely deformed patients may become disabled and form kidney calculi, causing gouty nephropathy. In addition, the increase of serum uric acid (SUA) level is also closely related to major systemic diseases in the human body. Some studies have shown that it has become the association for other major chronic diseases.5 The prevalence rate of HUA in developed countries is usually higher than that in developing countries, but since the beginning of the 21st century, it has been on the rise in various developing countries. As one of the largest consuming countries in the world, China has experienced rapid economic and social development in the past few decades. It is noteworthy that dietary patterns were associated to HUA in many research. The consumption of foods contained purine and alcohol, a fat diet and beverages could increase the prevalence of HUA.6 7

Alcohol consumption was the third largest risk factor of global poor health, one of the four most common modifiable risk factors, and the preventable risk factor of non-communicable diseases.8 Alcohol consumption had a very strong positive correlation with cancer.9 According to a study conducted in 2015, the current drinking rate of alcohol use among Chinese adults (aged 18 years and above) was 41.3%.10 Some studies have discussed the association between metabolic diseases and the consumption of alcohol, showing that drinking alcohol was correlated with blood pressure, glucose metabolism and triglyceride concentration.11–14 Considering the widespread alcohol consumption in the world, some clinicians' review of dietary advice for patients with gout and HUA shows that the most general advice is to completely restrict or avoid alcohol intake, in which this suggested frequency is usually beyond the weight loss.15 In the previous research,16 considering the type of drinking, beer seems to be the biggest risk factor, followed by spirits, while drinking wine only shows a slight increase in the incidence of gout, but it did not calculate the daily alcohol intake for different types of drinking. An Mendelian randomisation analysis in a group of individuals diagnosed with HUA and gout showed that an increase in alcohol consumption did not lead to gout.17 The influence of drinking frequency and degree on HUA needs further exploration. In recent years, the amount and degree of drinking are important related factors that scholars explore to affect HUA. To our best knowledge, few previous researches have explored the relationship between the frequency and degree of alcohol consumption and HUA in Chongqing, China. Therefore, based on the China Multi-Ethnic Cohort (CMEC) data, the purpose of this study was to investigate the relationship between alcohol consumption and HUA in Chongqing, China.


## Methods


### Study population

We obtained the data in Chongqing region from the CMEC. Previous researchers have provided information on the design of the cohort study.18 We recruited all participants randomly from 13 districts in Chongqing. The selection criteria were that they were over 30 and under 80 years old, had lived locally for at least 6 months, completed the baseline survey voluntarily and signed an informed consent document. The participants had normal expression and understanding abilities. Our study recruited 23 342 people aged 30–79 years. In order to explore the association between alcohol consumption and HUA levels, a total of 2509 individuals were excluded due to insufficient data on demographic characteristics, missing information on HUA status, incomplete data on alcohol consumption and missing physical examination results. In total, 20 833 individuals were included in the present analysis. The details of participant selection are shown in figure 1.


**Figure 1** Flowchart for participants’ selection. CMEC, China Multi-Ethnic Cohort; SUA, serum uric acid.


### Ascertainment of HUA

After overnight fasting, blood samples were collected by intravenous puncture from all participants, and then separated and used for future testing, including fasting blood glucose (FBG), blood lipids and SUA by centrifugation. The standard enzyme method for analysing uric acid was based on the use of fixed-enzyme-catalysed SUA production of hydrogen peroxide, which was then measured through a colour reaction catalysed by fixed-enzyme-catalysed oxidation. The testing variable coefficient for SUA in this study was <6.5%. HUA was defined as SUA levels above 417 µmol/L (7.0 mg/dL) for males and 357 µmol/L (6.0 mg/dL) for females, as reported in some study.19 20


### Assessment of alcohol consumption

The standardised Food Frequency Questionnaire (FFQ) was administered by trained research assistants during face-to-face interviews to gather data on dietary intake. Some questions were filled out by participants, ‘How often did you drink alcohol in the previous year?’ with four response categories: less than 1 day per week, between 1 and 2 days per week, 3–5 days per week or 6–7 days per week. If participants consumed alcohol less than 1 day a week, they would be further questioned about ‘Which of the following descriptions do you think is more suitable for you?’, with three response categories: 'abstainer,' 'occasional drinker ' or 'ex-drinker'. Finally, all the participants were grouped by six categories: 'abstainer,' 'occasional drinker ', 'ex-drinker', 'between 1 and 2 days per week', '3–5 days per week' and ‘6–7 days per week'. And if participants consumed alcohol more than 1 day a week, they would be further questioned about the following information: drinking experience in the past year, frequency of drinking and type of alcohol (beer, rice wine, spirits, high-alcohol liquor, low-alcohol liquor). According to the frequency and quantity of drinking answered by the current participants, the trained staffer would calculate the exact value of daily alcohol intake. The alcohol content in various beverage categories was determined as follows: beer contained 4%, rice wine comprised 18%, wine had 10%, high alcohol liquor accounted for 52% and low alcohol liquor represented 38%.21 Alcohol intake is calculated by converting the amount of alcohol contained in different types of alcohol into grams of alcohol, alcohol intake (g)=alcohol consumption (mL) × alcohol content (%) × alcohol mass fraction (g/mL), and alcohol mass fraction is calculated as 0.8 g/mL.22

According to the daily alcohol intake per drinking session and sex, participants were divided into four alcohol intake categories: moderate, excessive, high-risky and harmful. For the male population, if the daily alcohol intake is less than 25 g, it is defined as moderate drinking. If the daily alcohol intake is more than 25 g and less than 40.9 g, it is defined as excessive drinking. If the daily alcohol intake is more than 41 g and less than 60.9 g, it is defined as high-risky drinking. If the daily alcohol intake is more than 61 g, it is defined as harmful drinking. For the female population, if the daily alcohol intake is less than 15 g, it is defined as moderate drinking. If the daily alcohol intake is more than 15 g and less than 20.9 g, it is defined as excessive drinking. If the daily alcohol intake is more than 21 g and less than 40.9 g, it is defined as high-risky drinking. If the daily alcohol intake is more than 41 g, it is defined as harmful drinking.23 24 The details of classification criteria are presented in online supplemental table S1.




### Assessment of covariates

Detailed information about social demographic characteristics and lifestyle factors was collected by structured questionnaire survey. In addition to basic demographic statistics such as age, gender, marital status, education level, family annual income, the study also explored variables that would influence the prevalence of high uric acid levels, including smoking status, total energy intake, physical activity, alcohol consumption, dietary pattern, body mass index (BMI), high blood pressure, diabetes, dyslipidaemia and menopause (for women). All related covariates were introduced based on other research on the relationship between alcohol consumption and metabolic diseases.25 The physical activity of the individuals was quantified using metabolic equivalent tasks that simulated daily life activities (work, housework and leisure).26 The standardised FFQ included 13 major foods, which were completed by all individuals to obtain their dietary intake data. According to the dietary information in China Food Composition Table, the average daily caloric intake of each participant was calculated. BMI was calculated from the weight and the height. In our research, hypertension was regarded as mean measured systolic blood pressure or diastolic blood pressure of ≥140/90 mm Hg or a self-reported hypertension history diagnosed by doctor.27 Diabetes was regarded as FBG level of ≥7.0 mmol/L or a self-reported diabetes history diagnosed by doctor. Participants were defined as dyslipidaemia if they had any one of the four following components : triacylglycerol of ≥2.3 mmol/L; serum total cholesterol of ≥6.2 mmol/L; high-density lipoprotein cholesterol of <1.0 mmol/L; and low-density lipoprotein cholesterol of ≥4.1 mmol/L.28


### Statistical analysis

Participants in the study were divided into abstainer, occasional drinkers, ex-drinkers, 1–2 days per week, 3–5 day per week and 6–7 days per week according to the frequency of drinking and were divided into moderate, excessive, high-risky and harmful according to the degree of drinking.

Continuous variables were described by mean plus or minus SD, and classified variables were described by percentage. The differences between different variables and different frequency of drinking groups was compared by χ2 test and analysis of variance.

The relationship between alcohol consumption frequency and HUA, drinking degree and HUA was discussed by multivariate adjustment logistic regression model. The results were expressed by ORs and 95% CIs.

A series of adjustment models were employed to reduce the impact of confounding variables on this relationship. Model 1 was an original model without any adjustments; model 2 adjusted for age, sex (among total participants), marital status, total energy intake, physical activity, education level, smoking status, family income, more meat, more fish, less vegetables and less fruits; and model 3 additionally adjusted for BMI, hypertension, diabetes mellitus, dyslipidaemia and menopause status (among women).29 In previous research, the prevalence of HUA was found to be higher in males than in females,27 we would discuss these relationships in subgroups stratified by gender.

All statistical analysis and data management were done using software of professional SPSS Statistics V.26.0 and SAS (Statistics Analysis System) V.9.4. The level of significance was set at two-sided and a p value <0.05.


### Patient and public involvement

Patients and the public did not participate in the design, recruitment and implementation of research or the dissemination of research results.

### Association of alcohol consumption with HUA

We revealed a positive association between alcohol consumption frequency and HUA in table 2. After gradually controlling the potential confounding factors, in comparison to those who did not consume alcohol (abstainer), of current drinkers on 1 and 2 days per week, 3–5 days per week and 6–7 days per week, the adjusted ORs (95% CIs) were 1.31 (1.09 to 1.58), 1.51 (1.25 to 1.82) and 1.27 (1.07 to 1.50), respectively (p<0.001). Furthermore, multivariate adjustment analyses by gender stratification were carried out, and the result showed that males who consumed alcohol on 1 and 2 days per week, 3–5 days per week and 6–7 days per week had 1.35, 1.62 and 1.50 times likelihoods of having HUA, respectively (all p<0.001). But in the females group, compared with those who never drank, those relationships were not significant (all p>0.05). For the general population and men, compared with those who never drank, there was no significant change in the disease risk of occasional drinkers and former drinkers. But the HUA risk of weekly drinkers was obviously increased, among those the 3–5 days/week group had the highest rate of disease. Of current drinkers with 3–5 days/week, the adjusted ORs (95% CIs) were 1.51 (1.25 to 1.82) in the general population and 1.62 (1.32 to 1.99) in the males. In addition, there was no change in the risk of HUA with the frequency of drinking among women.

Similar significant positive relationships between the degree of alcohol consumption category and HUA are shown in table 3. In comparison to those who were abstainers, after adjusting for multiple confounding variables, the ORs (95% CIs) for those who drank alcohol excessively, high-riskily and harmfully were 1.59 (1.25 to 2.02), 1.49 (1.18 to1.89) and 1.81 (1.41 to 2.32), respectively (p<0.001). Furthermore, multivariate adjustment analyses by gender stratification were carried out, and the result showed that males who drank alcohol moderately, excessively, high-riskily and harmfully were 1.23, 1.78, 1.69 and 2.13 times more likely to have HUA (all p<0.001). But in the females group, compared with those who never drank, those associations were not significant (all p>0.05). For the general population, compared with those who never drank, there was no significant change in the disease risk of occasional drinkers, former drinkers and moderate drinkers, but the disease risk of inappropriate drinkers was obviously increased, among that the harmful drinking group had the highest disease risk (OR: 1.81, 95% CI: 1.41 to 2.32). For the male group, compared with those who were abstainer, there was no significant change in the disease risk of occasional drinkers and former drinkers. ## Discussion

This study investigated the relationship between alcohol consumption and HUA among adults in China. The frequency and category of alcohol consumption was positively associated with HUA. Consistent with some research, those people who drink more often have more possibilities to be middle-aged, males and current smokers, and they have higher BMI and total energy intake.30–32 After further adjusting for confounding factors, including age, gender, degree of education, income of families, the status of marriage, smoking, activity, energy, diet, BMI, hypertension, diabetes, dyslipidaemia and menopause (among women), there was still a strong correlation between alcohol consumption and hypertension.

We investigated the association of alcohol consumption with HUA among Chinese adults in Chongqing. Moreover, we found that both frequency and degree of alcohol consumption were positively related to the prevalence of HUA. Some researches met our findings. A cross-sectional study showed that drinking alcohol affects BMI and metabolically health, which would lead to high uric acid levels.33 A study consisting of Japanese metal product workers aged 20–54 years in Toyama prefecture, showed that heavy and moderate alcohol consumption would increase the rate of incident HUA.34 In the contrast, a study consisting of individuals aged 35 years or older on a rural general population in China, reported that moderate drinking alcohol did not increase the rate of HUA in men and women.35 In our study, we also found that inappropriate alcohol intake would increase the risk of HUA among general population, but moderate drinkers had no significant association with the risk of HUA. Furthermore, both heavy and moderate alcohol intake would increase the risk of HUA among men.

A prospective research showed that drinking alcohol would lead to HUA by increasing the synthesis of uric acid and blocking the excretion of uric acid.36 37 Some research showed that there was still an association between drinking alcohol and HUA in the analysis of dietary pattern stratification or gender stratification, which showed that drinking was an independent influencing factor.35 This large cross-sectional study focuses on the association between alcohol consumption and HUA and we need more arguments to verify this relationship.

Compared with those who never drank alcohol, individuals who consumed 3–5 days a week had the greatest risk of HUA. With the increase of drinking periods per week, the risk of HUA first increases and then decreases. The non-linear result of the relationship between frequency of alcohol consumption and HUA risk may be caused by the uneven distribution of drinking in this research and recall bias, participants did not want to admit that they drank more than 5 days in the past week. We would discuss the association between alcohol consumption and HUA in subgroups by gender. This situation may be due to the fact that male participants in our study prefer drinking heavily and frequently. At the same time, we should realise that anti-androgen therapy and female sex hormones can prevent the epidemic of HUA, which leads to a decrease in the probability of women suffering from HUA.38 39

In our study, drinking alcohol was significantly associated with HUA especially in men. Different from the benefits of moderate drinking found in other researches,40 41 the risk of HUA among moderate drinkers is 1.23 times than that of never drinkers among those in males. The risk of HUA is higher for those who usually drink alcohol than for those who never drink alcohol among the total population. Moreover, the risk of HUA caused by harmful drinking is the highest, which is 1.81 times than that of never drinkers among the total population and 2.13 times than that of never drinkers among males. However, among females, the risk of HUA was not found to change with drinking.


### Strength and limitation

Our study had several advantages as a large cross-sectional research discussing the association between alcohol consumption and HUA. First of all, this research selected a large sample of individuals in Chongqing. This area was famous for hot pot and had a very high rate of alcohol consumption every day, so the sample could be representative. Second, our research had a large sample of individuals and we adjusted for many confounding factors in a large range.42 This epidemiological study provided some explorations and thoughts about the relationship between drinking alcohol and HUA. Lastly, the determination of HUA required not only the patient’s self-reported diagnosis, but also the laboratory testing about individuals’ blood and uric acid levels. This criterion minimised the likelihood of overlooking HUA.

It should be noted that some limitations could be considered in this study. First of all, our cross-sectional research could not infer causality and did not distinguish the effect of other multiple factors related to diet from drinking alcohol itself. However, to improve the study, mixing factors were adjusted and subgroups were analysed. Further prospective and clinical studies in a large population were needed to verify this relationship. Second, standardised FFQ was used to obtain participants' drinking information, which may have reporting and recollection bias. Third, all individuals in this study were recruited randomly and voluntarily, which was more likely to be more educated and have a healthier lifestyle. And it might lead to underestimation of the prevalence of many chronic diseases. Fourth, our research did not consider the influence of medication information on HUA, for several types of medications, including diuretics, antihypertensive, allopurinol and uricosuric agents, can impact uric acid levels in the body. Finally, the current research only focused on Chongqing, in southwestern China, and could not represent the entire Chinese population. In the future research, it was essential to further validate the insights of this study in another large sample survey. More research was needed in multiple fields to explore the association between alcohol consumption and HUA.


## Conclusions

This current research had demonstrated that the frequency and degree of alcohol consumption are positively related to the risk of HUA. In addition, this positive connection was obvious among men, but weak among women. And moderate drinking was also found to be a risk factor for HUA among men in our study. Further prospective and interventional research was needed to verify the causal relationship and clarify the specific mechanism.
